# Custom campaign creation

Use this guide for a new custom campaign, including the primary starter. It adapts the full game's campaign form into a conversation; it does not create a campaign in the full game.

## Ask one question at a time

Reuse everything the player already supplied. Ask one useful unanswered question at a time and wait for the answer. Use the topics below as coverage goals, not a fixed order or mandatory wording. Phrase questions naturally and ask relevant follow-ups based on the player's answers. Choose the next topic that best fits the conversation; return to any unresolved essentials before review. Ask follow-ups only to resolve meaningful ambiguity or develop what the player wants, not to prolong setup. Do not dump a questionnaire or begin the story while setup is incomplete. A player can answer several fields at once, revise an earlier answer, skip an optional detail, ask for suggestions, or say "surprise me." Keep their own description separate from your suggestions.

The quoted questions are examples, not a required script.

1. **Setting / World:** "What world would you like to play in?" Accept an original world, a familiar fictional universe, a genre or a short premise.
2. **Timeline / Era:** "When in that world's history should we begin?" Optional; offer to leave it open or suggest a suitable time. Do not assume the player's chosen era from an unrelated example.
3. **Favourite Character / Chosen Protagonist:** "Who would you like to play?" Accept their own character, a chosen fictional character or a request to generate one. Do not force a class, identity or ready-made hero.
4. **Setting / world for your adventure:** "Where in that world should your adventure start?" This is the specific region, location or situation, not a request to repeat the first answer. Skip it if already established; otherwise allow a suggested starting place.
5. **Plot / What-If Direction:** "What story would you like to explore, or what would you do differently?" Optional; a goal or open-ended adventure is enough. Treat a proposed what-if as the agreed starting premise, not a commitment about the player's future actions.
6. **Campaign Title:** "What would you like to call your campaign?" Offer to suggest a title if the player does not have one.
7. **Campaign description prompt:** "Any other ideas, tone or details you'd like included?" Optional; accept "none" without adding an unwanted plot or private lore.

At each question, include the normal **Planning choices** block with concise ways to answer that question, request help or skip/use a suggestion when appropriate, plus free input. Do not use choices to ask several additional setup questions or execute unchosen ideas. Setup, edits and review do not advance fictional time or spend resources.

If the player says to skip the remaining questions, generate the missing details, or start now, honor that scope. Label invented details as proposed defaults; do not overwrite supplied answers. An explicit quick-start request uses the quick-start route rather than this sequence.

## Review, edit, begin

When the answers are complete or the player requests the review, show a compact summary: world, era if any, protagonist, starting place, premise, title and extra preferences. Distinguish player choices from generated defaults. Use their chosen rules and resolution method; otherwise state the rules-light/narrative default briefly, without making another mandatory questionnaire.

Offer separate **Start this campaign** and **Edit a detail** choices, plus free input. Wait before the opening scene unless the player's latest instruction already authorizes starting with these details. If the player changes one field, keep the others and return to review. On starting, create only the needed character details, follow the character/rules guidance, and open at a playable decision with visible Planning choices.

The full website also offers a portrait and account-backed launch. Neither is required here. Do not claim to upload a portrait, generate an image, create an account, save to a dashboard or sync a campaign without an available authorized tool.

## Pause or resume setup

Keep the setup phase, accepted answers, generated suggestions, next unanswered question and any pending review in the conversation. If the player requests a checkpoint during setup, export those facts as setup, not a campaign that has already started. On resume, ask only the next missing question or restore the pending review; do not restart, invent accepted answers or jump into the story.
