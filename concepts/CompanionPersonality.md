# Companion Personality

Every companion carries a personality tag and an alignment. You never see either one — they exist so the character stays the same person across a hundred turns.

## The personality tag

When the game generates a companion for you, the tag is a four-letter MBTI code:

- **I/E** — introvert or extrovert (where their energy goes)
- **S/N** — sensing or intuitive (how they take in information)
- **T/F** — thinking or feeling (how they decide)
- **J/P** — judging or perceiving (how they organise life)

An INTJ companion is introverted, intuitive, thinking, judging: a strategic planner who keeps to themselves.

The tag can also be a plain description instead — "mysterious and brooding" works as well as INFJ. Either way it drives the same thing: how the character talks and acts. A brave one states things flatly and does not hedge. A deceptive one deals in half-truths and watches your face. A scholarly one explains at length.

## Alignment

Alongside the tag, each companion has a D&D-style alignment:

- **Lawful Good / Neutral Good / Chaotic Good** — heroic
- **Lawful Neutral / True Neutral / Chaotic Neutral** — pragmatic
- **Lawful Evil / Neutral Evil / Chaotic Evil** — villainous

## The labels stay hidden

The game is instructed never to print an MBTI code or an alignment in the story text, and never to offer you a choice that would reveal one. You work out what someone is like by watching them, the same way you would with a person.

## Why the labels exist

They keep a character consistent. A companion you met eighty turns ago should still hedge the same way, still bristle at the same things, still make the same kind of call under pressure. The tag is the anchor that stops them drifting into a different person.

## Companion arcs

Every companion also carries a personal quest arc — four phases, roughly 20-30 turns, opened by turn 3. There is no approval meter. See [CompanionArc](CompanionArc.md) for the phases, the eight arc types, and how romance works.
