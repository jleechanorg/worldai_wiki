# Companions and player-led story

Use for recurring NPC relationships, companions, social conflict and optional personal hooks. These are portrayal tools, not mandatory arcs, relationship meters or a requirement to add companions to every campaign.

## Establish people, not services

Give a recurring character a concrete Want, Fear and Boundary, a recognizable voice and a role in the current situation. Add only details needed for play. Their loyalties, skills, limits, obligations and relationships should remain consistent with what has already happened. Use names and traits from the player's campaign, never private lore from another game.

A companion can have an independent goal while remaining useful and cooperative. They do not have to obstruct every plan to demonstrate agency. Let sensible agreements work. A current boundary may produce a refusal or counteroffer even with high trust; a specific meaningful event can change it, with that cause retained. Repeated persuasion, gifts or a high number do not automatically erase a conviction.

Keep authority clear. The player controls their protagonist and any allies assigned to them by the campaign agreement. Other companions make their own choices. A player can ask them for help, but the GM does not force the protagonist to accept it, speak, confess a secret, pay a debt or reciprocate affection.

## Make relationships specific

Track events rather than manufacturing a score:

- What this person wants now and what would change that priority.
- What they actually saw or heard, from whom, and how certain it is.
- What they offered, promised, refused or owe, and any stated conditions.
- What the player knows about them versus what remains unrevealed.
- Which observed event changed trust, fear, respect, resentment or willingness.

Two characters can interpret the same witnessed act differently. Distinguish personal concern, practical disagreement and moral conviction; do not make the narrator declare a universal verdict. Let warmth, humor, competence and ordinary conversation matter as well as conflict. An NPC may keep a secret without becoming a compulsory betrayal plot.

A bond is not a permanent obedience contract. Conversely, disagreement is not permission to punish the player with inevitable abandonment. Consequences should follow this person's motives, options and established boundaries. Allow repair, negotiation, changed plans and separation where plausible. Record absence, departure, hostility or renewed cooperation explicitly. A former companion continues as an independent person and does not silently rejoin the party after a scene change.

## Offer hooks, never force arcs

Draw possible hooks from established promises, places, goals and consequences. Present a concrete invitation or development when the fiction makes room for it, then let the player pursue, defer, redirect or decline. A decline does not justify recycling the same hook every turn or making it the only route forward. Preserve ordinary consequences of elapsed time if already grounded, not a punishment for refusing the GM's preferred story.

Do not impose a fixed number of companion appearances, a turn-count schedule, mandatory emotional revelations or a predetermined multi-act arc. Do not invent a tragic secret, romance, rivalry, betrayal or destiny solely because a companion has been present for several messages. Existing unresolved threads can remain unresolved. Prophecy, inherited rivalry, rulership and identity-defining obligations are invitations, not automatic assignments; refusal is not a moral failure or a reason to escalate pressure.

At a rest or lull, a short, optional exchange can respond to a recent shared event: a practical concern, a question, a joke or a disagreement. If the player wants to move on, move on. Do not interrupt a pending attack, reaction or urgent escape with a relationship scene. Downtime conversations consume fictional time only as declared or agreed, not because a rules/planning exchange occurred.

When the player chooses a personal objective, build from the resulting decisions rather than planning a compulsory endpoint. Record the next known opportunity and meaningful stakes. Resolve a completed goal honestly, including an early clever success. Do not withhold resolution just to extend an arc or give a companion control of the protagonist's climax.

## Dialogue and knowledge

Use distinct speech and behavior rather than biographies delivered without a reason. Let NPCs answer the actual question when they can; mystery does not require endless evasiveness. Do not have every NPC deliver exposition or mirror the player's beliefs. An attributed report can be mistaken, incomplete or deceptive, but do not retcon every useful answer into a lie.

A reveal reaches only those who perceive it. Record a listener's source and confidence, not a global suspicion value. An absent companion does not learn a secret from narration or a recap. If someone later passes the information along, preserve the messenger, opportunity and attribution. Corrections update what each recipient believes without erasing the original report from continuity.

The player character learns through their own perception, testimony or agreed abilities. A player-facing cinematic aside can reveal information to the player without giving it to the character. Preserve that distinction in later dialogue and decisions.

## Social stakes and outcomes

Before a consequential check, establish the actual request, listener, leverage, likely limits and resolution method. Persuasion is not mind control. Resolve success at a plausible scale: access with supervision, a temporary truce, a better price, a specific piece of information or an agreed next step. Honor a genuine success instead of moving the goalposts; decline impossible outcomes before rolling.

Do not test every friendly remark. If a relationship has earned easy cooperation and the request fits the NPC's interests, let it happen. If the request conflicts with a boundary, show the reason and possible alternatives without assigning the player's response. Continuing to argue is the player's choice, not permission to replay the same failed check indefinitely.
