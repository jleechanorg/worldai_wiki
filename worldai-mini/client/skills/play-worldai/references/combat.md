# Combat continuity

Use this reference for tactical conflict and combat-state repairs. The chosen rules and edition control mechanics. This is an execution discipline, not a new combat system. In narrative play, use only the state the fiction needs; do not impose initiative, HP or invented statistics on a rules-light game.

A quick uncontested action or isolated check does not automatically start a full initiative encounter. Use formal combat only when the fiction and chosen rules call for it.

## Establish the encounter

Carry the opening situation forward: who is present, what each side wants, relative positions, visible hazards, exits and the reason violence is occurring. Identify named participants consistently, including distinguishable members of a group. Describe material constraints before they affect a decision. Do not turn investigation, a threat or an ambiguous gesture into the player's attack.

If the chosen rules require initiative, surprise, distances or a map, establish them using the agreed method before their consequences matter. Ask for a consequential missing value instead of fabricating an official rule or an existing character statistic. You may create new adversaries and scenery as GM within the chosen fiction, but do not retrofit their capabilities after seeing the player's tactic to defeat it. Do not reveal a hidden enemy or statistic merely to fill a template.

Maintain a compact record, using known values and marking unknowns:

- Encounter objective, time/place and current phase: active, paused or ended.
- Round, turn order and current actor when that system uses them.
- Each combatant's identity, side, status and relevant position or distance.
- Current/max HP or equivalent resources; defense and modifiers only when established and needed.
- Conditions, durations, concentration or comparable maintained effects, and their rule-defined expiry triggers.
- Remaining actions, movement, reactions and other limited options for the current turn or round.
- Pending action, attack, damage, saving throw, reaction or choice, with its target, agreed difficulty and already-applied costs.
- Encounter rewards or resource changes already applied.

A short player-facing status line is often enough. Keep hidden facts out of it. Persist accepted combat continuity in model-authored player state using mini_save_state; claim a save only after its receipt.

## Resolve one sequence cleanly

1. Confirm the current actor and the player's exact declared action, including target. Preserve actions already taken this turn. A menu suggestion or feasibility question does not consume an action.
2. Check established range, line of effect, terrain, conditions and remaining resources. If illegal, explain the specific obstacle before spending a resource. If multiple legal interpretations materially differ, ask the player which they mean.
3. State the required roll, modifier, target/success bands and stakes before seeing the result. Use the agreed resolution method. Pause for player-reported rolls. Do not substitute invented dice or silently treat a requested roll as narrative resolution.
4. Resolve each dependent stage under the selected rules: an attempt is not a hit, a hit is not damage, and damage is not necessarily unconsciousness or death. Do not ask for duplicate attack or damage results already supplied. If a remaining result is missing, pause at that stage and preserve the completed stages.
5. Apply costs and outcomes at the rule-defined time, once. Update HP, limited uses, conditions, concentration, position and other affected state together. Record what was paid even if the attempt fails. Never charge the same spell, ammunition or action again when a response resumes.
6. Handle triggered saves, reactions and interruptions in the correct order. When the player has an eligible reaction, present the trigger and relevant known cost before proceeding past the decision. Give using and declining the reaction separate numbered options in the Planning choices block, so selecting a number identifies one decision. Do not automatically spend a limited reaction or select a character option for them. If they previously gave a clear bounded standing combat instruction, follow it within its scope.
7. Give other combatants their legitimate turns. NPC allies and enemies have separate resources and actions; a party member's success does not resolve everyone else's turn. NPCs choose plausible actions from their own motives and knowledge. If the player controls allied characters by agreement, pause for those choices rather than taking over.
8. Advance the actor/round only when the current sequence is resolved. Reset a resource only at its established reset point. End at the next meaningful player choice or unresolved roll; do not skip several risky turns to speed up narration. Always append the core skill's visible **Planning choices** block. Put a pending roll or reaction first; all other options must respect the current actor, unresolved stages and remaining resources. Asking a question or describing another plan does not cancel or replay an already-resolved stage.

Narration and the mechanics line must agree. A stopped bleeding condition is not healing unless the rules say so; zero HP is not automatically death; surrender is not an attack; a fleeing enemy does not vanish from a reachable battlefield. State uncertainties instead of filling gaps with precise invented numbers.

## Tactics, fairness and completion

Use terrain, cover, noise, visibility, morale, objectives and the costs of an approach when established. Opponents can protect someone, seek an object, negotiate, withdraw or surrender. Their tactics use information they possess, not private player planning. Telegraph perceivable dangerous preparation before its consequence when fiction allows. Do not secretly add resistances, reinforcements or immunity solely to negate success.

A powerful character may win easily against an ordinary foe. Preserve that outcome. Challenge can come from an informed specialist, protecting vulnerable people, competing objectives or a meaningful cost, not automatic stat inflation. Any such pressure needs a plausible cause and should leave meaningful choices.

Conclude when the encounter's conflict is actually resolved: opposition defeated, escaped or surrendered, the objective settled, or both sides disengaged. Do not keep combat active solely to fill a turn quota, and do not erase still-relevant pursuit or injury. Summarize survivors, known positions, lasting conditions, expended resources, recoverable items and unresolved threats. Grant eligible rewards once, marking pending versus applied. Return to ordinary scene play at a genuine decision.

## Resume and repair

A mid-combat resume preserves round, actor, spent movement/actions, active conditions, costs already paid and pending checks. Do not reroll initiative, restore resources, replay a resolved hit or regenerate a defeated enemy because a checkpoint was pasted.

For a contradiction, use the latest explicit correction and trace affected state. Example: if damage was accidentally applied twice, restore only the duplicate subtraction and repair dependent unconsciousness/turn claims. If several corrections are possible and choosing would alter a player's decision, ask rather than selecting a convenient history. Explain the minimal repair. An out-of-character correction does not itself advance combat time.
