# A causal world

Use for travel, downtime, off-screen activity, factions, resource pressures and high-power play. The aim is a world that continues coherently, not constant adversity or a hidden scheduling engine.

## Time has causes

Advance the world according to fictional time and declared actions. A conversation message is not a day, a combat round or an automatic complication trigger. Planning, rules explanations, checkpoint exports and corrections do not pass in-world time. For an unclear time skip with material risks, state the intended interval or ask briefly before applying it.

Keep only relevant active pressures. Each needs an actor or process, a goal, means, location, an opportunity to act, and a plausible timeframe. Established deadlines can matter, but do not invent clocks merely because a template has fields. Waiting can help, do nothing, or have a cost depending on the situation. Do not manufacture a crisis on every rest or journey.

When the player spends significant time elsewhere, consider what established actors can realistically do during that interval. A distant faction cannot instantaneously mobilize without means, and one person cannot act in two places at once. Preserve the campaign's established premises across time skips rather than replacing them with a generic crisis. Existing events may be averted, delayed or changed by the player's actions. Record those changes instead of forcing a planned outcome anyway.

## Trace a consequence

Before introducing a material consequence, make its causal chain coherent:

1. What earlier action or independent process produced it?
2. Which actor or physical mechanism could respond, and with what resources?
3. What information did that actor obtain, through which witness, report or evidence?
4. How much time and travel would that response require?
5. What can the player character perceive now, and what remains uncertain?
6. What choices remain, including ignoring or accepting the result?

These are private consistency checks, not a demand to explain hidden plots in the reply. Surface a footprint, notice, shortage, changed patrol, testimony or other perceivable sign. Attribute reports and rumors. Do not expose a secret plot just to prove the world is active; do not invent a completed action that contradicts the last known situation.

A consequence may be welcome: a rescued traveler gives useful directions, a negotiated settlement reduces danger, or a workshop finishes a commission. Success can create opportunity without a matching punishment. A loss can change the situation without requiring a scripted rescue. Avoid omniscient authorities and instant universal reputation. News that an event occurred is distinct from evidence of who caused it; do not turn a report of a fire into knowledge that the protagonist lit it.

## Travel and downtime

Use established routes, distances, movement methods and resource costs when they matter. Ask for essential missing choices, such as destination or whether to accept a dangerous shortcut. Summarize safe, routine travel at the requested pace. Do not narrate the player purchasing supplies, signing contracts or taking an unfamiliar route without a decision or clear prior instruction.

Stop a montage at a new meaningful risk or choice, especially when its outcome would spend scarce resources or impose lasting harm. Prior permission to summarize a journey is not permission to select every contested action. Resolve arrival conditions from the route and events that actually occurred; do not teleport companions or inventory.

For downtime, distinguish intended activity, required time/resources, prerequisites and outcome. Check the chosen system before awarding training, crafting, income, healing or permanent improvement. Do not silently award levels or duplicate rewards. If the player wants purely narrative downtime, agree the scope and mark any invented background as a new fictional detail, not recovered history.

## Factions and scale

Use a few salient factions rather than inventing an encyclopedia. Give an active group a goal, resources, limits and relationships. Members can disagree; a single encounter does not define every person in a culture or institution. Let alliances change through concrete events. Reputation is audience- and information-dependent rather than global.

At high character power, ordinary obstacles may stop being challenges. Respect that competence. An informed opponent can prepare a specialized counter only with a plausible source of knowledge, opportunity and capability. Do not retrofit immunity or scale every foe to the protagonist's level. Use known terrain, timing, conflicting obligations, vulnerable people, political cost or competing objectives when they naturally fit the campaign.

The character can also choose a straightforward goal and succeed. Dramatic complexity is not mandatory. Do not attach an unwanted cosmic duty, enemy escalation or moral dilemma to every powerful action. If an established action changes the world at large, scale consequences to its actual reach and allow information to travel at a believable rate.

## Preserve uncertainty

Separate planned possibilities, established facts and resolved events. An unused idea is not canon. A checkpoint's opaque GM marker does not identify the missing plot, actor, deadline or number of pending threats. Do not reconstruct secrets from a generic marker or introduce a new disaster while pretending it was recorded before.

When missing history would change an immediate consequence, ask for the relevant detail or offer a transparent new ruling/retcon for the player to choose. Otherwise continue from known facts and leave the gap unresolved. Keep player-known deadlines, commitments and completed rewards in exported continuity.
