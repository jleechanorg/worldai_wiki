# Companion Arc

Each companion carries a personal storyline that runs alongside the main campaign. The first one opens by turn 3, and a full arc takes roughly 20-30 turns from hook to resolution — the game will not let one wrap up early.

## Structure

Every companion arc runs through the same four phases.

| Phase | Typical turns | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** | 3-8 | The hook lands — the companion acts strangely, or something from their past surfaces |
| **Development** | 9-18 | Complications deepen it and the stakes rise |
| **Crisis** | 19-26 | Failure becomes genuinely possible; ultimatums arrive |
| **Resolution** | 27+ | The confrontation, and a lasting change to the world |

The first companion's arc opens by **turn 3**, and no later than turn 5. After that, arc beats land every turn or two — including on turns when nothing else in the world is moving.

## The eight arc types

Every companion arc is one of eight kinds, chosen from that companion's backstory:

| Arc | What it is about | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Personal redemption | Atoning for a past mistake | Victims resurface, moral choices arrive |
| Lost family | Searching for a missing relative | Clues, rumors, old acquaintances |
| Rival nemesis | Hunting, or being hunted | Agents, bounties, ambushes |
| Forbidden love | A romance with complications | Letters, secret meetings, jealousy |
| Dark secret | Hiding something dangerous | Near-discoveries, blackmail |
| Homeland crisis | Their home region in danger | Refugees, news, desperate messages |
| Mentor legacy | Finishing a mentor's work | Old contacts, unfinished business |
| Prophetic destiny | Marked for something greater | Visions, zealots, prophecy |

## What companions actually track

There is no approval meter. A companion is an NPC, so they carry the same hidden **trust** score as anyone else — -10 to +10, described in [NPCRelationships](NPCRelationships.md) — plus their one personal arc.

In play, that means companions react to what you do through trust and through their story, not through a bar you can farm. A companion can refuse an order that violates their values, interpret it their own way, or act on their own loyalties. A full betrayal is possible, but the game requires it to be built up over several turns rather than tripped by a score crossing a line.

## Callbacks

Every arc beat plants at least one **callback** — a condition that pays off later. Callbacks fire on entering a place, after a number of turns, on meeting a certain kind of person, during a particular fight, or while resting somewhere specific.

That is why an arc feels like a chain rather than a run of disconnected scenes: a pendant noticed on turn 4 becomes a sailor's remark in a port on turn 8, becomes a slaver trail on turn 12, becomes an ambush on turn 18, becomes a confrontation on turn 25. Going where a callback points is how you push an arc forward.

## Romance

Romance is not a separate system and there is no approval bar to fill. It arrives one of two ways: the companion's arc is a **forbidden love** arc, or the story goes there because you steered it there.

How explicit it gets is an account-wide setting, not a companion stat: the 🌶️ switch beside the campaign title in the game header. It applies to every campaign on your account, not just this one. With [Spicy Mode](SpicyMode.md) off, intimate scenes play as tension and fade-to-black, and the game may occasionally suggest turning it on. With Spicy Mode on, scenes are written out. You can play any companion arc start to finish without pursuing romance at all.

## Your character gets one too

Companions are not the only ones with a personal storyline. Your own character receives exactly one, opened by turn 3 on the same schedule and built from your backstory, stated goals, or class identity rather than a random plot. It runs through the same four phases and plants the same kind of callbacks. The difference: its subject is not drawn from the fixed list of eight — it is invented from who your character is.

## Player tips

- **Follow the callbacks.** When a companion reacts to a place, an object, or a person, that is a planted hook. Going back to it is what moves their arc.
- **Answer companion requests.** When a companion's request calls for an answer from you, the game must offer at least one way to respond on that same turn. Ignoring it costs you trust.
- **Give it room.** Arcs need roughly 20-30 turns and cannot be rushed; the game will refuse to resolve one early.
- **There is no bio screen.** Everything you know about a companion comes from the story text. What they say and how they say it is the only readout you get.
