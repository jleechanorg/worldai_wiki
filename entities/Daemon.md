# Daemon (Prince Daemon)

A charming, dangerous antagonist archetype: commanding, witty, philosophical when it suits him, and entirely willing to use violence. He is best known from House of the Dragon, and he is the pivotal NPC in the ready-made [House of the Dragon campaign](../queries/house-of-the-dragon-campaign.md), where you play his unacknowledged bastard.

The game does not ship Daemon as a built-in character. You get him by writing him into your campaign premise — which is how you get any named character from a story you love.

## In the House of the Dragon campaign

Hegemon of the Crownlands, rider of Caraxes, and by far the most dangerous person who might be on your side. He has not acknowledged you in writing and has not denied you either; he sent you a black cloak without a word and left it there. You are a test he is running, and he is entertained by both outcomes — legitimizing you, or letting the Greens finish you.

That ambiguity is the campaign's engine. Every scene at Harrenhal is a chance to resolve it in your favour or to remind him you were never worth the trouble.

## The archetype elsewhere

- **Wants**: power, legacy, freedom from anyone else's rules.
- **Voice**: dry, provoking, occasionally grandiose.
- **Method**: charm first, blade second, apology never.
- **Loyalty**: real but conditional — he is with you while your goals point the same way.

## How the game plays a character like this

Every recurring NPC carries an internal personality profile — a temperament tag, an alignment, and a want/fear/boundary set — that drives how they decide and how they speak. You never see the labels: the game is instructed to keep them out of the story text entirely, so you read Daemon the way you would read a person, by watching what he does. See [CompanionPersonality](../concepts/CompanionPersonality.md) and [NPCRelationships](../concepts/NPCRelationships.md).

Because the profile is stable, a Daemon-type NPC stays in character across hundreds of scenes. That is what makes him predictable enough to plan around and dangerous enough to be worth planning around.

## Player tips

- **Don't take the first offer.** A high-charisma NPC is persuasive precisely when the deal is bad.
- **Match his energy.** Boldness earns his interest; hedging earns his contempt.
- **Predictable is not simple.** He will keep his stated goals and break your expectations about how he reaches them.
- **Antagonists can become allies.** Relationships shift when your goals overlap — an enemy with a shared enemy is a temporary friend.
