# Character Archetype

> This is general tabletop vocabulary, not a WorldArchitect.AI setting. The game never asks you to pick a party role and stores no such field — only your class and subclass. Use these labels to decide which class you want; nothing in the game reads them.

A shorthand for the job a character does in a fight.

The game ships full rules text for **one subclass per class** — the SRD one, named in brackets below. Ask for another (Battle Master, Oath of the Ancients, Gloom Stalker) and the GM will usually build it from its own knowledge, but the numbers are less reliable than the SRD subclasses.

## Tank

Front-line fighters who soak damage and hold enemy attention. High HP, high AC.

- **Fighter** (Champion)
- **Barbarian** (Path of the Berserker)
- **Paladin** (Oath of Devotion)

## Striker

Big single-target damage, usually dependent on good positioning.

- **Rogue** (Thief)
- **Ranger** (Hunter)
- **Monk** (Way of the Open Hand)
- **Warlock** (The Fiend)

## Controller

Area denial and battlefield manipulation — crowd control, area damage, environmental effects.

- **Wizard** (School of Evocation)
- **Sorcerer** (Draconic Bloodline)
- **Druid** (Circle of the Land)
- **Warlock** (The Fiend)
- **Ranger** (Hunter)

## Support

Healing, buffs, debuffs, and utility.

- **Cleric** (Life Domain)
- **Bard** (College of Lore)
- **Paladin** (Oath of Devotion)

## Classes that do two jobs

Three classes appear twice in the lists above. Paladins tank and support. Rangers deal damage and control the battlefield. Warlocks do the same, leaning on a reliable damage cantrip alongside their control spells. If you want to cover two roles at once, start with one of those three.

See [CharacterCreation](CharacterCreation.md) for how to build one, and [Subclass](Subclass.md) for what a subclass changes.
